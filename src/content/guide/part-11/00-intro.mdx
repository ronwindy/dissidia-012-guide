# Chapter 2

Chapter 2, and now for another party!

#### Onion Powah!

I like that D012 made the fairly simple alteration of putting the storylines in a sensical, somewhat chronological order, rather than just leave you to figure it out yourself. Anyway, character time.

---

<CharacterIntro name="Onion Knight" src="drake/Dissidia_OnionKnight.png" width={175} height={268} alt="Dissidia OnionKnight">

The Onion Knight, much like the Warrior of Light, has the problem of being an original character. They made him more memorable though, he's a cheeky wimp who is actually quite cowardly and foolish, and puts up an act of being brave and intelligent to hide it. He's given a pretty weak character arc as a result, but even if his development isn't all that interesting, he's still amusing and memorable, in part to a good VA performance from Aaron Spaan.

The Onion Knight is a fast and agile character, armed with fast-hitting low-range sword attacks and slow long-range magic attacks. His attacks don't do much damage though, but he makes up for it with HP links.

Uniquely, while other characters learn their HP links by mastering appropriate attacks, the Onion Knight learns BRV to BRV attacks, then once he masters the BRV link, he learns the HP link. This means mastery of his attacks costs less AP, though totalled with the BRV link it's about the same as other characters, and his attacks can all link to Bravery or HP attacks, like Blizzard to Blizzaga or Quake. This goes for all his Bravery attacks too.

His HP attacks are slow to start but are similarly divided into sword and magic. He later learns "booster" abilities to give him temporary boosts to his attack, defense and critical hit rate for either magic or physical attacks. There's not much to talk about when it comes to what his attacks do, you start out with all four of them and what you see is what you get, they're pretty straightforward.

The Onion Knight's problem is that he's incredibly predictable, because aside from his link attacks, he has only 8 attacks:

- 4 Bravery: two in the air one each for melee and magic, two on the ground for melee and magic
- 4 HP attacks, same distribution

This means at most times he only has four attacks available to him, and three of them - the spell and HPs - are slow to start up while the melee one is easily blocked. This unfortunately makes the Onion Knight one of the weakest characters in the game.

He's not one of my #4 that I never use, but he is certainly one of my last choices aside from them for a fight. Against the AI they're of course too stupid to realize his flaws, but I'll reckon human opponents are no threat. But if you're really good at faking out the opponent and playing mind games, I guess give him a shot.

As I've said before though, this walkthrough is only concerned with story mode, not human opposition. In that regard the Onion Knight is perfectly capable, though still low on the ladder and outclassed by many other characters.
