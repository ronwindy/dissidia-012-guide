# Prologue

Part 9, with the cycle beginning anew in Round 13, the fighting at last draws to a close.

#### Preparations

In the aftermath of the 012 cycle, we'll get the Level 20-29 booster, but it's a 1.5 and isn't as good as the first two.

I'm also close to getting the Earring and Muscle Belt, lacking a few Champion's Desire and 1 each of the Sephira and Girl's Dream. It's okay though:

- During Chapter 1 we'll get the last bit we need for the Muscle Belt.
- In Chapter 2 we can get the bits for the Earring, and I'll note my accessory set-ups appropriately.

---

We'll also get a Lucky Charm, we can get more after the 013 story. For now, if you have three Wanderer's Dream and three Voidshards (and you should, if you don't you will get them soon), if you can the charm in for a Rabbit's Foot.

It increases Luck by 8 and also causes Summons to recharge two bars faster, effectively meaning Ifrit for example will recharge after only one fight, and Cactuar will charge after only two. It's very helpful, if you like using summons then ditch one of your lower-power boosters to use it. Up to you.

---

Go to the PP Catalog, as usual buy upgrades to the Calendar for icon likelihood and AP bonus, and once they're all bought buy the gil.

More importantly though, under the System tab buy "Trade Component Drop OK". This will allow you to win items from enemies beyond their basic Battlegen items, including the Battlegen items for other characters and just general trade accessories.

Under the ETC tab you can buy the Black Chocobo and Fat Chocobo Play Plans:

- Buy and switch to the Fat Chocobo course, you won't get exp bonuses for fights (not a problem since level grinding is easy in this game) but you will win Chocobo items more often, giving you free money whenever you need it, and later on letting you level grind more efficiently, since otherwise to get the three types of items you need to switch between the three plans since each only awards one item type.

There's various other new stuff available but nothing needed, spend your remaining PP on calendar stuff and exit.

#### Awakening

Our character for the prologue, brief as this will be, is the Warrior of Light. It doesn't matter how you suit him up, the prologue is short and when we come back to him the Warrior will be using new equipment.

Launch the story now. Advance warning, for some reason dumb reason SE did not redub the cutscenes for the original game, so the 013 story's voice acting is a step or two down. The cutscenes are also pretty bland and static compared to 012, I can get not wanting to redo them all over but you had all the original VAs back anyway, at least re-record the dialogue, how long could it take, really?

I recall an interview where James Arnold Taylor said he had his work on Tidus done in a day.

The Warrior of Light, along with the Onion Knight, is an original character created solely for Dissidia, based on the artwork for the original Final Fantasy but having no character background to base him off of. As a result he's pretty bland but this is arguably the point.

He's not prone to angsting or contemplating his naval in indecisiveness like the other cast members, he's just devoted to Cosmos and will do whatever he has to to protect her. As the later Reports in D012 will show, there's a reason his personality is so flat, and when you connect the Reports, to the main story, to the ending of the 013 cycle, you get a pretty creative character arc.

The Warrior is a pretty balanced character, he last the power, speed, and technicality of others, but makes up for it with versatility. He has short and mid-range combo attacks with decent power and several HP links, and versatile HP attacks.

His initial attacks:

- Dayflash, a quick three-hit slice that begins a chase
- Blue Fang which calls a hail of ice on the opponent, good for setting up combos

His aerial attacks:

- Crossover, a spinning forward shield attack with an upward stab to finish and begin a chase
- Shield Strike, a downward shield toss before he dives and knocks the opponent in the direction he came from

HP attacks:

- Shield of Light, a short-range blast of light that also defends from attacks, and he automatically aims at the opponent when using it
- Shining Wave, sending a burst of light energy at the opponent and tracking them into the air and through walls

Shield of Light is the best one, the others have long-start up and AI opponents later in the game will dodge them easily, the Shield though will stagger them as it blocks their attack and leave them vulnerable.

Overall the Warrior is a good character, not great, but not bad, well-suited for the basic hero archetype. Use Blue Fang (and later White Fang) to set opponents up for combos and then dash to them and use Crossover or Dayflash, depending on your preference.

When he begins to master his Bravery attacks and pick up his HP links, rely on them to whittle down the opponent's HP faster than his slow and limited HP attacks.

Overall though, as I said he's balanced, other characters are faster, or do a lot more damage, or are just more fun to play as. But if you like him, enjoy him.
