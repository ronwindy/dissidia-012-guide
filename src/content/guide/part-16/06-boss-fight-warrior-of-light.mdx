<PanelSection kind="enemies">

The Warrior comes into battle with a Flametongue, giving him an initial Bravery boost on top of his level advantage. He's got a pretty nice accessory build, 3 Attacking Bravery and 4 Near Opponent pumping up a Power Ring. I'd have opted for a Hyper Ring or a Muscle Belt, but hey, it's decent.

He's got:

- HP links for all his aerial attacks and his ground attack Ascension
- Radiant Sword to send six swords of light homing in on you
- Shield of Light to block and punish your Bravery attacks
- Dayflash as a quick ground attack to get you out of his face

His summon is...I'm not really sure, he never used it.

</PanelSection>

<PanelSection kind="tips">

The problem is, as always...yup, EX Mode. Use that new Thunder Barret to drag him in and slam him with an EX Mode Solid Barrel to Break him, then enter chase and wall rush him to knock off 2000 HP within the first ten seconds. Now just land another Solid Barrel, HP attack and EX Burst to end it.

The Warrior is decently aggressive and has a good set-up for moves and equipment, he just can't stand up to the brute power of Squall's EX Mode bonus.

</PanelSection>

<PanelSection>

Squall got to Level 12 from this fight and learned Fated Circle, a ground version of Aerial Circle that creates orbs horizontally. It combos well with Thunder Barret as I said.

</PanelSection>

</BossFight>

---

The Warrior is impressed with Squall's power, and wonders why he doesn't use that power to help protect his friends. Good question, since Squall knows other villains are actively pursuing them.

Squall responds by saying that even if they're apart, they can support each other, and shows him Bartz's chocobo feather, claiming that they support him in their own way and he's returning the favor. That...really doesn't answer the question, and is a weak argument in itself.

The Warrior sees he has misjudged Squall, thinking he preferred to be alone because he didn't trust his allies...which was never the case, and Squall never said so. The Warrior departs, reminding Squall he isn't alone, something Squall clearly already believes, no matter how bullshit an argument he formulated to express this. Why didn't they just have this chat before the fight? I dunno, like I said kiddies, this chapter's morals are all screwed up.

---

Remember kiddies, if your friends are being pursued by an androgynous mage intent on trapping them, it's best to ignore the danger to them and just have faith that they'll be okay, because they have faith in you. That's the *true* meaning of friendship.

And now because I know you were all thinking it - "Squall, don't believe in yourself, believe in them! Believe in the friends that believe in you!"...ya know it makes a lot more sense phrased as Kamina said it. I guess the shades just have that effect.
