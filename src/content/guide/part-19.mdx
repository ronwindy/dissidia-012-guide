PLAYABLE CHARACTER - **Warrior of Light**

<Figure side="left" src="drake/Dissidia_Warrior_of_Light.png" width={150} height={258} alt="Dissidia Warrior of Light" />

The **Warrior of Light**, along with the **Onion Knight**, is an original character created solely for Dissidia, based on the artwork for the original Final Fantasy but having no character background to base him off of. As a result he's pretty bland but this is arguably the point. He's not prone to angsting or contemplating his naval in indecisiveness like the other cast members, he's just devoted to **Cosmos** and will do whatever he has to to protect her. As the later Reports in D012 will show, there's a reason his personality is so flat, and when you connect the Reports, to the main story, to the ending of the 013 cycle, you get a pretty creative character arc.

The **Warrior** is a pretty balanced character, he last the power, speed, and technicality of others, but makes up for it with versatility. He has short and mid-range combo attacks with decent power and several HP links, and versatile HP attacks. His initial attacks are **Dayflash**, a quick three-hit slice that begins a chase, and Blue Fang which calls a hail of ice on the opponent, good for setting up combos. His aerial attacks are Crossover, a spinning forward shield attack with an upward stab to finish and begin a chase, and **Shield Strike**, a downward shield toss before he dives and knocks the opponent in the direction he came from. HP attacks are **Shield of Light**, a short-range blast of light that also defends from attacks, and he automatically aims at the opponent when using it, and Shining Wave, sending a burst of light energy at the opponent and tracking them into the air and through walls. **Shield of Light** is the best one, the others have long-start up and AI opponents later in the game will dodge them easily, the Shield though will stagger them as it blocks their attack and leave them vulnerable.

Overall the **Warrior** is a good character, not great, but not bad, well-suited for the basic hero archetype. Use Blue Fang (and later White Fang) to set opponents up for combos and then dash to them and use Crossover or **Dayflash**, depending on your preference. When he begins to master his **Bravery** attacks and pick up his HP links, rely on them to whittle down the opponent's HP faster than his slow and limited HP attacks. Overall though, as I said he's balanced, other characters are faster, or do a lot more damage, or are just more fun to play as. But if you like him, enjoy him.

First comes a **False Hero** with Crossover and Radiant Sword. Next it's a **False Stalwart** with **Round Edge**, Twin Swords and **Earthquake**. Third is a **Delusory Warlock** with **Rising Wave**, **Float System** and **Nightglow**. Now comes the Hard enemy. He's got a **Hyper Ring** with two After 15 Seconds boosters, 3 After 30 Seconds, 2 After 45 Seconds, and one each After 60 Seconds and After 90 Seconds. Thus, the longer it takes you to kill them, the stronger they get, and those are permanent boosts once they go off. After just 30 seconds it's 4.8, but at 45 seconds? **19.2**. Yeah, kill these guys quick because that's almost a 100% damage increase at 45 seconds...and then the After 60 Seconds booster activates to triple it. And 90 seconds...well if you let them get to that point, you deserve the sound thrashing they'll inflict on you.

They don't waste any time at all, eh? **Garland** isn't some wimp at **Level 10**, even if you're only at **Level 3** he's not holding back...well he is somewhat, but he's still pretty tough. He has no summon or Assist, fortunately. His accessories consist of a **Hyper Ring** with 4 HP = 100% boosters. Landing an HP attack to deactivate them should be your first priority of this conflict. Otherwise he has the typical moveset, and good **Bravery** due to his level advantage, though fortunately his equipment isn't as awesome as usual and he has no shield. As a result, once you get those boosters turned off, he isn't too tough, **Break** him, wall rush and then whittle him down.

I used the **Warrior**'s **EX Mode** here so might as well cover it to fill space. The **Warrior**'s **EX Mode** is a class change to a Knight, recovering his shiny knightly armor with red and yellow highlights and tilting the horns down to point forward for some reason. As a Knight the **Warrior** has Protect cast on him, giving him an extra 10 defense points, at this stage of the game that's as good as an extra shield equipped, and even in the late game is effective. Second, he gets the Mirror Attack ability, automatically protecting him from low-priority attacks while attacking himself. Mostly this just works out to reflecting magic since melee moves will bounce off each other like normal, but it's appreciated. His third bonus is the Light's Blessing ability, which summons blades of light energy whenever he uses a melee **Bravery** attack to strike the opponent and do more damage. The individual swords have low power but he summons about a dozen or more of them each time, and while the resulting boost isn't as awesome as **Terra**, **Cloud** or **Squall**, he still sees a noticeable spike in his damage output aside from the critical hit rate boost. The **Warrior**'s **EX Burst** is quite easy, press each directional command as it appears on the screen to blast the opponent with slashes infused with light energy.

**Warrior** hit **Level 4** from this fight and learned Sword Thrust. It's a mid-ranged attack that begins with a shield throw, then he charges in for a sword combo. It has slight reach into the air and can't be blocked, blocking it staggers the opponent and leaves them vulnerable to the follow-up sword attack.

As **Garland** kneels and fades, he and the **Warrior** exchange some generic banter, the hope of **Cosmos** is just an illusion and it's foolish to chase it. This is one thing that helps make the **Warrior**'s badassery shine through, so many villains appear to the heroes and taunt and demoralize them, but the **Warrior** always replies to the effect of "bitch please, you're evil and I'm awesome, I'm too busy thinking of what angle to jam my sword up your ass once you shut up to care what you're actually saying". That said, **Garland** fades and the **Warrior**'s journey continues.

As we reach the end we find **Firion** tired from the fight with **Sephiroth**. The **Warrior** sends **Firion** off and takes **Sephiroth** off alone, once again ignoring **Sephiroth**'s little taunts and just saying "let's get this over with".

On the one hand, **Sephiroth**'s level and equipment are on-par with **Garland**'s last time, and we just cut down another gateway of enemies to level up a couple times. He shows the reason **Firion** chased him - the wild rose, of course - and takes off when he senses **Cloud** approaching. The **Warrior** wonders what **Sephiroth** meant, but yet we must continue on. Take it from me dude, listening to **Sephiroth**'s ramblings can only lead to madness, just tap X until he shuts up and goes away.

The second Hard enemy is an **Imaginary Brawler** fond of **Moonsault Kick**, **Burning Arrow**, the same timed boosters, and the **Marilith** summon. **Marilith** is the most dangerous of the four fiend summons since it freezes your **Bravery** on a short 5-second timer, and the AI will call it once you land an HP hit, leaving it likely you'll be frozen at low **Bravery**, so quickly go on the offensive so at least you can spam decent HP attacks once the freeze kicks in. The last enemy the **Cross Chain** misses is an **Ephemeral Phantom** with the midair **Jecht** Block and **Jecht** Blade.

The **Warrior** hit **Level 8** during this round of enemies and learned White Fang. It's an excellent replacement for Blue Fang, sending out a wave of lightning bolts forward, and can set the opponent up for melee moves. It's not as awesome as **Squall**'s **Thunder Barret**, but it works well enough. There's also a nifty glitch you can try out with it while in **EX Mode** - use White Fang, then use a melee move as it hits the opponent. If you time it right, the swords of light from the melee move will appear to strike the opponent, and depending on the move, bring them towards you. Yes it's technically cheating as a glitch, but I say hard work should be rewarded and it's tricky to pull off.

The chest has a piece of Gold in it.

The **Warrior** once again tells her to move out of the way or take a sword to the face. Guess which option she picks. Hope the infobox doesn't spoil it for you.

**Ultimecia** has significantly more dangerous equipment set-up than **Sephiroth** or **Garland**. First, she's a ranged fighter, so she has that advantage even if the AI is bad at using it. She also has a much better weapon then they do, giving her almost an extra 10 attack points. Her defense is the same though, the Poncho cancels out the boost she gets from her Bronze Bangle and level. She uses the Bomb summon, which you haven't seen in some time. When she calls it the Bomb builds up energy before exploding, sapping **Ultimecia**'s **Bravery** to 0 and inflicts damage to your **Bravery** equal to hers, Breaking you if your **Bravery** is below **Ultimecia**'s. Her accessories are an **Earring** and **Attractorb** with six Far From Opponent. I'd toss in a **Pearl Necklace** or a second **Earring** but that set-up is workable.

Ultimately though, while **Ultimecia** has better gear, she still has weak defenses and is easy to **Break**, which coupled with her AI makes her a pushover. The only annoyance is that it's hard to wall rush her since **Shield of Light** is so slow and doesn't stagger her when it blocks her moves, and Crossover for a Chase puts her up into the banish trap. Fortunately you have **Dayflash** which begins a chase by knocking her backwards, letting you get into a Chase without knocking her into the trap. From there do the usual pattern, **Break** and wall rush, let her recovery, **EX Burst** to finish her off.

**Ultimecia** can't take the hint however and continues taunting him after the fight. This time however it slightly works, as she mentions all his comrades are doubting themselves, so how can he trust in them? She then takes off, and the **Warrior** once again continues on. We then see a shot of his meeting with **Squall**, but for some reason we don't get to do the fight from the **Warrior**'s perspective.

After that a helpful narrative screen mentions his meeting with **Squall** as he continued on into the desert, headed to the **Land of Discord**.
