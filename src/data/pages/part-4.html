Chapter 4 - And now for a more personal story arc.

Stay Away From The Summoner!

Another new character.

PLAYABLE CHARACTER - Yuna

Howdy Yuna, what's shaking?. Yuna isn't my favorite character, but she's certainly not on my list of dislikes. She's got a good character arc in her base game, isn't too annoying (though that may be because Wakka raised the bar for shithead-ery), and is a Summoner, and Summoners are up there as one of my fav character classes. Sure, she's no Rydia or Garnet, but them's some tough competition for anyone. In this game among the six heroes, Yuna is pretty bland though, between Laguna and Vaan being comic relief and Kain and Lightning being stoic badasses, Yuna is just sort of there. In this chapter she gets a much more personal quest than the rest of the group, and in terms of the singular stories of the chapters, I would say it has the best one, and connects very nicely to the 13th cycle. But that's for another part in the future.

In battle, Yuna doesn't fight herself, she calls Aeons to attack for her. And to my great disappointment, it is nowhere near as badass as I'd expect from a Summoner. Yuna has only six Bravery attacks, and they're divided up the same way for ground and air - close-range melee attack, charging mid-range attack, and long-range projectile attack. I find thus that she's very boring to play as, her moves are good for sure, they pack a fair punch, especially in EX Mode, but when there's only four worth using since the projectile attacks suck, it doesn't make for engaging and fun combat. And since she's just starting out she won't learn those charging attacks for several levels, making fighting as her even more boring because she now has basically only two attacks. She does have one combo using the Holy glitch - fire off a charge Impulse, and if she recovers before it hits, time your usage of Heavenly Strike as the opponent is hit by Impulse, and even though you didn't hit with Heavenly Strike, the game will let you combo it due to the glitch since Impulse hit. You can practice this throughout the story if you like.

Her HP attacks are better varied, Yuna is an AI Breaker, the computer opponents even in the hardest areas of the game seem incapable of dodging Energy Ray, and Thor's Hammer and Hellfire are nice close-range moves with good radius. Eventually Yuna gets Mega Flare which breaks the AI even harder. And...that's it. Yuna only has two moves worth using until she gets Sonic Wings at Level 12, so run up to an enemy, slam them with Meteor Strike or Heavenly Strike until you can kill them, then fire off an Energy Ray and do so. Nothing else to say, she really can't do anything else yet, and even later there's not much more to it. I have no idea why they made her so limited, FFX had plenty of Aeons and that means Yuna has plenty of potential attacks since she uses the Aeon's moves, so where are Yojimbo and Anima?

Before we begin, I confess I did a few Sephiroth vs Kefka matches in Quick Battle to get enough Harlequin's Dreams to get a Sunrise. I only did this though because the game's natural story encounters allows enough to get one by this point, I was just unlucky, that's the only "cheating" I'm doing though, I'm not gonna go fighting Garlands to get a Hyper Ring because the story hasn't given us enough False Stalwarts to fight for the same yet. I used Sephiroth because we use him in a Report later so he can use the levels, and as a villain it won't skew the difficulty in any later storylines because he won't be used in any. My equipment set-up for now thus is a Power Ring and Sunrise with the Level 1-9 Booster, and the Red Drop because I had a spare Rosetta Stone from last time. You'll also notice as a nod to X-2, Yuna can equip guns. That Valiant is perfect for her, and next chapter when we go back to Lightning, she can use it too. In fact of the six heroes of this storyline, four use guns, and of the two that don't one isn't playable in the last chapters, so guns are pretty much the go-to weapon to prioritize getting since almost everyone can use the guns you buy.

As you can tell clearly, a Straight Chain will chain the four enemies here, if you have one. Jecht is our Assist and he's one of the best Assists in the game, his aerial Assist does high damage and slams the opponent into the ground, and it's long enough that if you're close-enough to the opponent when he starts it up, you can hit with Heavenly Strike and recover in time to use Thor's Hammer on the enemy before they recover. His ground Assist is less useful since it slams the opponent up into the air, but a well-timed Heavenly Strike will drive them back down,and depending on the arena they may not be so high as to be unreachable. Also, for most of this storyline we have a lot of new opponents! Let's do a rundown.

The Ephemeral Phantom is Jecht's Manikin, it uses Jecht's ground combo attack Jecht Rush, the midair Jecht Blade which is a charging series of sword swings, and Jecht Block which deflects all moves. Yuna will stagger if he deflects Meteor Strike, but her other moves either cause no stagger or stagger him too, so rely on aerial strikes to fight him. There's an Imitation Despot which you'll recognize from a Report, they have Land Mine which puts so mines on the ground, Meteor Strike will dispel them, and Light Crest which creates an energy field that fires projectiles that bounce off obstacles when you get close. HP is the aerial Flare. The Ephemeral Summoner has the same Meteor Strike and Heavenly Strike as you do, but a Diamond Dust HP attack in the air which has shorter range than Thor's Hammer but executes a lot faster and is hard to anticipate, so be on your toes or only strike when she's grounded. And then there's a Delusory Dragon....you know them by now I'm sure.

And to cover it because hey, this is a guide, the enemies of course have new Battlegen items to win. The White Cape, which gives a magic defense bonus, might want it sometime, can be traded for three Magicite Shards, three Despot's Hopes, and three Flash's Hopes. If you got lucky enough in Chapter 2 to get some Girl's Dreams, the Silver Hourglass which increases EX Mode Duration by 5% needs three Girl's Dreams, three Phantom's Dreams, and three Blackcrystal Slivers, we've got plenty of the last one, since it seems every other battle so far has been in the Chaos Shrine. There's nothing else of note though, still a lot of items needing Battlegen stuff we can't get yet.

The chests have the Cotton Robes and Hairband for equipment, and a Guard Ring, a nice accessory but not anything we need, I prioritize power in my builds. As we leave, Jecht suddenly vanishes and Yuna finds herself alone. Thinking back on something from home, she whistles, and suddenly and impossibly, Tidus comes running by! She calls out to him but Tidus doesn't recognize her and runs off. Time to get to the bottom of this mystery.

The Pilgrimage

On the world map we start on a beach (nice mythology gag) and the path forward out into the marshlands is linear. As you come out and grab the second orb of the dead, make a left to a chest with an EX Charge and a Counterfeit Wraith to fight, she has Anti-Air Particle Beam. Continue across the marsh to a ruined castle with a Moogle shop. On my game I had 195 KP, almost enough to get my hands on those coveted 200 KP items! If you have enough now, maybe it's your Bonus Day and you've got a discount or had a Chain Skill I lacked and didn't have to buy one, if you have 200 KP and feel the natural urge to splurge, buy the Icebrand, as Swords are one the most versatile equipment types, usable by a good portion of the hero characters, and the Icebrand is the most powerful sword we can use until we get to Level 30. Vaan, Laguna, Lightning and Kain use Swords, so this weapon will see more than its share of usable throughout the rest of the game, probably going to end up being used by all my characters throughout the 013 story except for Terra since she can't use swords (I don't know why, she could in FF6, but that's not something we need to get into here).

For the other items available, none of them are must-buy items, but Empty Assist Gauge would be the best if you must, since the other four have fluctuating activation requirements, EAG, you'll always know when you have no Assist and thus the gauge will always be empty during that time. As an edit note, while writing this walkthrough I did not buy EAG and later wished I had, so yes, buy it, learn from my mistakes. Summons are all a waste of KP at this point. Buy a Multichain for down the road, we may get one from the orbs of the dead but it's unlikely. The stone produces a grind bar leading to a Rosetta Stone. As we approach the next gateway you'll see Tidus turn and run behind it.

This gateway is one of the precious few times a Round Chain has any use, and I happened to get one from the orbs on my way here. You can use a Multichain at C4 if you want, you'll make 16 KP for the chain and can go but another Multichain when you're done, but I was cheap and decided the Round Chain would lead to larger net winnings, being that it was free and didn't need replacing. I used it at C4 to chain the three, then manually chained the last two. Zidane is our Assist, his ground attack is Rumble Rush and is a combo that launches the opponent into the air, his aerial attack is Vortex which much like Jecht's ground assist slams the opponent up and probably out of reach. Heavenly Strike slams the opponent down so stick to the ground Assist for Zidane. You'll also find a new arena here, Dream's End, which FFX fan will recognize easily I should hope. It's a big wide-open arena with a banish trap pit on most of it, you can fight on the central large platform or the outlying grandstands, and railings along the outside let you quickmove between the two.

Enemy line-up, the Ephemeral Phantom, Ephemeral Summoner and Fleeting Flash are familiar. The Imitation Despot is a problem though, he's got Thunder Crest and Dyanmite, remember them from the Report? Except his HP attack this time is the ground Flare, which sends out a large fireball that slowly tracks us around the stage for a long long time, making it a constant danger, and if you get caught in one of his Bravery attacks that stuns you, there's a good chance Flare will come around and explode in your face as you writhe trapped. Don't let this happen. Other characters can deflect it to track and hurt him like Laguna's Ragnarok Blade or Lightning's Crushing Blow. Yuna has no such attacks to do this yet, sadly. There's another new enemy, the False Hero. He has Dayflash on the ground, a quick three-hit combo, and Rising Buckler in the air which is an upwards-swooping attack that hurts a lot, listen for his cry to block it if you're above him. His HP attack is Shield of Light, he blocks and fires a blast of light at short-range, but our HP attacks will crush it and Heavenly Strike won't stagger us for getting blocked.

The only trick here is the enemies have summons. The Flash has Odin, summoned when you Break her, and it might Break you back. The Despot has Bahamut for periodic boosts of 75 Bravery, and the Phantom has Demon Wall to protect his Bravery from damage when he gets low on it, fortunately you'll have no trouble building up Bravery to kill him without needing to Break him. With the first round of enemies dead, the chest has an Oak Staff, useless for Yuna with the Capella on her belt, and the Ramuh summonstone. Ramuh summons himself automatically when we get Broken (and if you're getting Broken at this stage of the game, you're doing it wrong), and seals the opponent's summonstone (not that enemies at this stage of the game really use them too often, and again, if the ones that do are Breaking you, you're doing it wrong). So yeah he's pretty useless. All automatic summons have manual versions, and the difference is usually the automatic ones have more charges and recharge faster while the manual ones let you decide when to use them, and which of the two is stronger depends on the exact summon, but Ramuh is one of the ones that you never want the automatic version for.

Zidane runs off when we get to the exit, I guess he realized Yuna has a boyfriend so hitting on her is wrong, and we're alone again on the world map.

Now, there have been exactly two before, and by this time a Battlegen item should be available for win, Stalwart's Hopes, you have to Break them to get it. As always, into the blue gateway, kiddies!

Enemy line-up this time is two Idle Sky Pirates, an Ephemeral Phantom, a Counterfeit Wraith, and a Fleeting Flash. One of the Idle Sky Pirates is perhaps our most dangerous foe yet. He begins with 3000 Bravery, uses the Tiamat summon to triple it up to 9000, and then spams Windburst, and Yuna has nothing that can reflect it yet. I would say this is the first battle so far where Carbuncle is useful, except that as a Strange Battle Piece he has low HP so once your Bravery hits 0 from the HP attack he'll be dead. Instead try Cactuar, wear him down as much as you can before Tiamat finishes its countdown to activation and then summon Cactuar if you get him to say, 1100 or lower, leaving him with a few hundred once Tiamat goes off. If you can't get him that weak then don't use Cactuar, save the charges. Use a Jump Chain where you start to chain four enemies, I lacked such though so I had to fight them one by one, a Multichain would only get 3, meaning we get 9 KP for a skill we have to pay 10 KP for.

The chest only has a Guard Stick, bleh, useless. It unlocks the Mythril Rod in the shop which we can't get because we have no Mythril. As I said before, D012's main problem when it comes to its shop and equipment system is they don't give you the trade accessories needed to trade for equipment until the point that equipment is outdated. There's a lot of enemies here but don't be intimidated, we can handle them. First fight the Imitation Despot at D4, then move there and use a Multichain to chain six enemies and win a lot of KP. Feel free to use that EX Charge and take damage in the fights, that Potion there will let us fill our HP and EX to full before we face the boss. Speaking of EX Mode, time to chat about that I guess. Yuna's EX Mode increases her damage output to insane levels, when she uses a Bravery attack two Aeons appear to attack at once. Her EX Burst has commands appear as rings of light close around them, press the shown command just as the ring closes to be just around the command icon, repeat several times. During this round of enemies Yuna got to Level 11 and learned a new HP attack along the way up, Hellfire. It creates three columns of flame in front of her with a pretty high reach, it's effective at close-range and can deflect those attacks like Flare and Windburst on start-up, but otherwise stick with Energy Ray.

The enemies are two Ephemeral Phantoms, two Ephemeral Visions, two False Heroes, an Imitation Despot, and an Ephemeral Summoner, all enemies you've seen before and can handle. The Phantoms have Triumphant Grasp, a downward-arcing charge HP attack that can easily be avoided by just getting above them, and the Visions have Energy Rain. One of them is the Strange Battle Piece and has Odin along with 3000 starting Bravery. Cactuar is good if you have charges for it. The False Heroes have an HP attack called Radiant Sword, it creates six swords of light that fire outwards and spread out, then turn and rush you to impale you. It can't be blocked and the swords will deflect your projectiles, so you'll have to dodge them. They have quite a bit of range on them too. As I said, if you take an HP hit or two, it's fine.

The chests have a Pink Tail (that's six) and a Soul of Valor. Chug the Potion and approach the Chaos Piece. Yuna finds Jecht and Tidus squaring off and intervenes. Seems the Warriors of Chaos got Tidus here to take down Jecht and have brainwashed him. Ya know, if you wanted to recruit characters to take out Jecht, why on earth would you go for Tidus? I guess it makes sense, he did kill Jecht once already, but he sort of had help. Now Hades, there was a man with taste in minions, why settle for the rest when you can have Auron? Yuna tries to get through to Tidus, encouraging him to remember her and Jecht, and Tidus seems to begin to wake up when the Emperor appears and launches off a Flare at Yuna. Tidus takes the hit and gets blasted, and the Emperor sneers down at Yuna for screwing up the fight he had set up. Yuna orders Jecht to take care of Tidus while she handles the Emperor. Gotta admire her guts, she tells Jecht to stay back.

Well as the choice of boss music will remind us, Yuna has experience taking out smug fashion victim mages who won't stay dead. You've already seen most of the Emperor's moves before with the Imitation Despots, but he's smarter about using them - if he snares you in a Thunder Crest, he will take advantage of your vulnerability. To refresh your memory further - Dynamite fires an orb that sticks to obstacles and draws you in to stun you, Thunder Crest creates a lightning field on the ground to do the same, Landmine and Mine toss out exploding orbs of lightning that can be dispelled by dashing through them, and Light Crest creates a field that shoots projectiles when you get close. His new Bravery attack Bombard is a close-range staff smack that knocks you back, but you should never be close enough when he's not being hit himself that it can hit you. He has both versions of Flare, the ground one that follows you and the midair one that stays in place. There's a way to deal with the blue Flare now, lure it into tracking you through a floor or wall, like the giant sword in the middle of the stage or the railings along the edges, and it'll explode when it hits the obstacle. The explosion can still hit you though so stand clear.

His new HP move is Starfall. When he begins to charge it up it has one of the largest charge times in the game, several seconds, but when it goes off you're screwed, a giant meteor is going to slam down on your head preceded by fireballs, and unless you can quickmove away, you are not going to be avoiding it. When charging he's immune to projectile attacks, and unfortunately, Heavenly Strike, strictly speaking, has the same damage priority as a projectile, so the only way to break through his defenses and stop him when he begins Starfall is to use Thor's Hammer or an EX Mode Heavenly Strike, which also calls Ifrit to do a different type of damage that will hit fine. And to wrap up this description, he has the Giant of Babil summon, which saps 30% of your Bravery and his and adds it to the stage pool, and he has accessories for a 10% buff to his magic damage when he's far enough away from you.

With his high defense (for this point), your Bravery attacks won't do as much damage as you're use to, so use Yuna's EX Mode buffs, or Heavenly Strike to knock him down in the banish trap over and over and whittle him down. He can be surprisingly tough, with that HP he can endure two Break-boosted HP attacks and keep coming, and his high level means he'll have a base Bravery boost to over 400 points, so Yuna's 2000 or so HP will melt off pretty quick, especially since his aerial Flare inflicts wall rush. His weakness is fortunately he isn't that smart about using his traps, just smarter than the Manikins, if he played like a human player - that is, Thunder Crest and Flare the place up constantly to keep you from getting close to him - he'd be a real challenge. He still is, for a new player at least, but only because his higher level gives him a power bonus. You can win as long as you aren't stupid and don't blunder into his traps too much.

In the aftermath of the fight, the Emperor observes Tidus and Jecht laying unconscious, and notices Jecht transferred his power to Tidus to save his life at seemingly the cost of his own. Deciding to use this to his advantage, he takes Jecht's shell of a body away and vanishes, leaving Tidus to Yuna. Laguna and Vaan show up and head off again. We unlock a new Report as always, but this one has no fights and is just a cutscene so I'll let you watch it without summing it up. When we open the menu we'll also be notified that we get a new accessory for completing Chapter 4, the booster Level 10-19, it's also a 1.5 and guess when it activates. Makes an easy and quick replacement for Level 1-9 when you outgrow it. Sadly, the boosters for higher level ranges are only 1.2, but for now these two are great.
