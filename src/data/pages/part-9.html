Preparations

In the aftermath of the 012 cycle, we'll get the Level 20-29 booster, but it's a 1.5 and isn't as good as the first two. I'm also close to getting the Earring and Muscle Belt, lacking a few Champion's Desire and 1 each of the Sephira and Girl's Dream. It's okay though, during Chapter 1 we'll get the last bit we need for the Muscle Belt, and in Chapter 2 we can get the bits for the Earring, and I'll note my accessory set-ups appropriately. We'll also get a Lucky Charm, we can get more after the 013 story. For now, if you have three Wanderer's Dream and three Voidshards (and you should, if you don't you will get them soon), if you can the charm in for a Rabbit's Foot. It increases Luck by 8 and also causes Summons to recharge two bars faster, effectively meaning Ifrit for example will recharge after only one fight, and Cactuar will charge after only two. It's very helpful, if you like using summons then ditch one of your lower-power boosters to use it. Up to you.

Go to the PP Catalog, as usual buy upgrades to the Calendar for icon likelihood and AP bonus, and once they're all bought buy the gil. More importantly though, under the System tab buy "Trade Component Drop OK". This will allow you to win items from enemies beyond their basic Battlegen items, including the Battlegen items for other characters and just general trade accessories. Under the ETC tab you can buy the Black Chocobo and Fat Chocobo Play Plans, buy and switch to the Fat Chocobo course, you won't get exp bonuses for fights (not a problem since level grinding is easy in this game) but you will win Chocobo items more often, giving you free money whenever you need it, and later on letting you level grind more efficiently, since otherwise to get the three types of items you need to switch between the three plans since each only awards one item type.

There's various other new stuff available but nothing needed, spend your remaining PP on calendar stuff and exit.

PLAYABLE CHARACTER - Warrior of Light

The Warrior of Light, along with the Onion Knight, is an original character created solely for Dissidia, based on the artwork for the original Final Fantasy but having no character background to base him off of. As a result he's pretty bland but this is arguably the point. He's not prone to angsting or contemplating his naval in indecisiveness like the other cast members, he's just devoted to Cosmos and will do whatever he has to to protect her. As the later Reports in D012 will show, there's a reason his personality is so flat, and when you connect the Reports, to the main story, to the ending of the 013 cycle, you get a pretty creative character arc.

The Warrior is a pretty balanced character, he last the power, speed, and technicality of others, but makes up for it with versatility. He has short and mid-range combo attacks with decent power and several HP links, and versatile HP attacks. His initial attacks are Dayflash, a quick three-hit slice that begins a chase, and Blue Fang which calls a hail of ice on the opponent, good for setting up combos. His aerial attacks are Crossover, a spinning forward shield attack with an upward stab to finish and begin a chase, and Shield Strike, a downward shield toss before he dives and knocks the opponent in the direction he came from. HP attacks are Shield of Light, a short-range blast of light that also defends from attacks, and he automatically aims at the opponent when using it, and Shining Wave, sending a burst of light energy at the opponent and tracking them into the air and through walls. Shield of Light is the best one, the others have long-start up and AI opponents later in the game will dodge them easily, the Shield though will stagger them as it blocks their attack and leave them vulnerable.

Overall the Warrior is a good character, not great, but not bad, well-suited for the basic hero archetype. Use Blue Fang (and later White Fang) to set opponents up for combos and then dash to them and use Crossover or Dayflash, depending on your preference. When he begins to master his Bravery attacks and pick up his HP links, rely on them to whittle down the opponent's HP faster than his slow and limited HP attacks. Overall though, as I said he's balanced, other characters are faster, or do a lot more damage, or are just more fun to play as. But if you like him, enjoy him.

Once the Despot is down (use Shield of Light to reflect Flare, but get some distance since the light blast to reflect it doesn't come out for a couple moments), follow the path to the ruins of Cornelia, grabbing the orbs of the dead of course. There's a chest in the ruins with an EX Charge. I set the clock ahead to avoid my bonus day, so he had some limited items for sale. Banish/Death is largely pointless, we want to fight to level up. Behemoth and Pupu are both awesome, Behemoth doubles your Bravery instantly then drains it for a period of time, excellent for powering up a final blow, and Pupu halves your Bravery then steadily increases it, good for use after an HP hit so the penalty doesn't hurt you. His Boosters are good, Pre-EX Mode and Pre-EX Revenge are 1.4 and 1.3 boosters that will almost always be active in story mode, though Armorless is useless for now. He also sells various Elixir items needed to trade for trade components in the shop. I would buy Pre-EX Mode if you can afford it, if you can't get, Behemoth. Too poor for that as well, like me? Buy a Dusty Elixir. Or maybe you can't buy anything, his inventory depends on when your bonus day is so perhaps you can't.

Approach the gateway and you'll be informed of one of D012's dumbest mechanics, the Bonus Line. All gateways in all stories now have a bonus line, if you're over it you get a KP penalty at the start of negative KP (you don't lose KP for exiting with a negative penalty though). By contrast, lower your level to below the line to get a bonus to KP without fighting. The dumb part comes in with the fact this is only here to encourage players who import their Dissidia data with level 100 characters to lower their levels to the bonus line to be challenged...except you don't have to, and later in the game you can earn thousands of KP at a time, so players may not even care. On the other hand the bonus line applies regardless of data import, so if you're playing on a bonus day with an Exp bonus or are just good at level grinding and say, bring your Level 11 Firion to a Level 8 gateway, you're encouraged to set his level to 8...except while you lower your level this way you don't increase in level while fighting, so your Firion may gain enough Exp to get to Level 14, but he'll remain at Level 8. And no, you can't change level in a gateway, and when you finish it you have to manually set your level back to normal, it's really tedious and annoying. I would have just added in an option so when you enter a gateway it asks "Change Level To Bonus Line? Yes/No", and it automatically changes your level or not to the bonus line, and then you include the third option to manually set level, and the level is changed only for that gateway. So much simpler and convenient.

There's another new sight here, though it doesn't carry over to replays of the 012 stories - Emblems. Emblems come in Red, Blue and Purple varieties and active when you fight while standing on them (enemies do not get their effects if they're positioned on an emblem). Blue emblems grant you various boosts, like stopping breakable accessories from breaking, boosting critical hit rate or restoring your HP before the fight. Red emblems inflict penalties, like negating your equipment, making certain attacks do no damage, or starting you with 0 Bravery. Purple emblems are "neutral" and will affect both or affect neither and do something else, like increasing the KP you win for the fight, raising the stage Bravery, or raising the initial Bravery of both characters. In this guide, I'll mention ones I use but you can hover your mouse cursor over the emblems on the map to view what each one does.

The enemies here are a False Hero and Fleeting Flash. The Hero has a Delusory Dragoon Assist and the Flash has an Imaginary Brawler. When facing both you'll get an emblem to boost the critical hit rate of your attacks. They won't take long to kill with that boosting you, but they're a bit smarter then you might expect for the Prologue, the difficulty level from the 012 story hasn't reset. The chest has a Purple Drop which boosts Regen by 5%, barely worth existing. I got into EX Mode during the second fight, but since that's the last time we'll use the Warrior until Chapter 10, we'll chat about his EX Mode during that time.

As we reach the end the Warrior finds Cosmos, weakened from exerting so much power at the end of the 12th cycle.
