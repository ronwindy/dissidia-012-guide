Chapter 9, the penultimate quest, and I'm sure you know who's turn it is.

The Mouse Trapped

Say hi to our latest hero.

PLAYABLE CHARACTER - Bartz

Ah, Bartz. FF5 is an interesting game, the only things people know it for are Gilgamesh and the awesome Job system. But it never had anything really resembling a coherent three-act plot with any sort of even flow to events, or anything resembling deep and memorable characters. The Japanese developers apparently agree because Bartz's story is completely bland, he's a happy-go-lucky guy who loves adventure, but he's a bit of a dummy and a little cowardly at times. The moral lessons he learns from his story is to not run from your enemies...even if it is you against innumerable odds. At least that's the only lesson I ever saw him learn, and it makes no sense, but why break a streak of nonsensical aesops?

Bartz's fighting style is pretty lame too. He's a Mime, so he doesn't use any attacks of his own, he takes attacks from other characters and combines them to make his own, like charging forward with Cloud's Climhazzard, then punching the opponent away like Tifa's Beat Rush. In that regard though his moves kinda suck, he has a handful of close-range moves and a few slow long-range moves, and his HP attacks are likewise, he has four, melee and long-range. So, much like Yuna, Bartz suffers from an extreme lack of reliable moves that makes him, to me, rather un-fun to play as. He's not weak, his moves do pack a good punch to them comparable to Squall or Cloud, but he's just boring to play as, for me at least. His moves have the unique property of possessing support skills, in the end-game he'll learn stronger versions of those abilities for himself, or can have equipment to provide those boosts, but they're still nice add-ons, and some stack with the innate ability for a higher boost than normal characters can get.

Bartz begins with Blade Crash, a two-hit sweep with Lightning's Gunblade, stepping forward slightly, then he slashes past the opponent like Vaan's Katana and begins a Chase. His Reel Impulse throws an axe like Firion's Reel Axe, but then he jumps up and attacks to wall rush them with Zidane's Mage Mashers. For aerial moves he has Rush Impact, where he slashes twice with Kain's lance as in Lance Barrage, then slams the opponent down like Lightning's Smite. His other aerial starting move is Holy, which works just like Terra's but sucks. No, really, it has weaker homing and speed. His HP attacks are Yuna's Hellfire and Laguna's Ragnarok Blade, which work just like the originals.

As I've said so often, Bartz is fine for his own story but I dislike using him in later stories due to his limited moveset and lame non-melee moves.

I bought some new armor for Bartz, an Enyo's Breastplate just because I was getting tired of always using that Silken Shirt. Also an economical purchase, everyone except Cecil and Terra can equip it, and in the original six 012 heroes, all but Yuna and Kain. I traded in two Dusty Elixirs to get the Bangaa Tooth needed. So he's got the Chakram, Sakura, Green Beret, Enyo's Breastplate, and the typical accessories - Hyper Ring, Muscle Belt, Sunrise, HP 100%, Level 1-9. Bartz won't have an Assist for some time so give him Empty Assist Gauge.

As the story begins, Bartz hides from Kuja and Kefka as they come looking for him and begins looking around while he's here.

On the up side it means they won't be able to use EX Mode, but their absorption range of EX Force means you probably won't be getting any for yourself

Golbez has one of the dumbest builds of any boss since Ultimecia and her trio of Cursed Rings. He has a Hyper Ring with seven HP = 100% boosters, and thus his build will drop to useless the second he takes a single point of HP damage. He's also got Odin to strike us with Break at a 50% success rate, but it's the Auto version so it only appears once he's at low HP, and since we'll probably be ending this fight with an EX Burst as per tradition, Breaking him in the process, Odin is useless because summons can't be triggered during a Burst.

As a result of the above, Golbez is a pretty lame boss. They tried to make him tough, but it fails. He has the same attacks as always so the same strategies as always will carry you through to victory, he seems a little smarter than usual, but is still an idiot. The problem is that unless the extra damage from completing the attack will Break me, or if you've got an Assist to combo with, I don't get why you would never not want to use HP links, doing HP damage is always better than doing Bravery damage, and if you're inflicted with Break an HP attack will cure it. The AI never got this concept and thus Golbez doesn't abuse his four HP links like he should. And Gravity System, well, the AI never got the idea that it's ridiculously easy to counter, either.

Well, gotta fill up this empty space next to that big boss template, let's chat about Bartz's EX Mode. He masters his Mime job, gaining a longer red cape and three golden stars over his head as in FF5. His EX Mode bonus is disappointing though, it's the Goblin Punch attack. Pressing R and Square will use it, it's a combo of hits to do Bravery damage and then launch the opponent away, it has low range but very fast execution. The reason it's lame is because the Bravery damage it does is pathetic normally. The true power of Goblin Punch is that when used on an enemy with the same level as Bartz, its Bravery damage increases eightfold, and suddenly it does as much Bravery damage as Bartz's Bravery combos. Awesome yes, but outside of player-versus-player matches, how often will Bartz and his opponent be at the same level? Even in the end game when he's Level 100, enemies go past that. Bartz's EX Burst is a little tricky and needs good reflexes, you have to press three pairs of directional pad commands as they appear to have him bombard opponents with eight magic-empowered slashes, before he combines his weapons into his own Brave Blade and delivers the final strike.

With the Badass in Black beaten again, he gives more ambiguous advice before there's an earthquake and Bartz takes off while Golbez is distracted. Maybe he should have just come out and told Bartz the Crystal is a trap, but that might make too much sense. We also get an odd scene of Golbez and Exdeath, Exdeath hints Golbez is gunning to get his own Crystal, but nothing ever comes of this, a pity too.

As always, Exdeath could be one of the most challenging opponents yet, save for one trait. He's got a great accessory line-up, an Attractorb, Earring and Guardian Bangle pumped up by 7 Near Opponent boosters. Thus his Hurricane has a big boost in damage, and every time you attack him with a melee move - which of course is most of yours - he's going to get a decent defence boost. He has that Brynhildr summon again, and a Fleeting Flash Assist - contrary to the prior cutscene, Squall is still hanging around to be yours. And since last time in Cecil's story he was just a mid-boss, he's a lot tougher than last time we tangoed with him.

Not much I can say about him otherwise, you've fought him three times by this point, you know all the annoying tricks he has, you know that his biggest weakness is the poor AI. Exploit his Reverse Polarity spam as last time, attack while he's on equal footing to bait him into using it, neutral dodge and he'll appear in front of you, a sitting duck. He's got Turn Guard on the ground, Reel Axe can crush it but Hazard Raid is asking for him to block it, and in the air it's Omni Block. Overall, you can handle him, his blocks are annoying but you know how they work for yourself. His only new trick is Malestrom, an HP attack that has him charge for a long time, then summon a whirlwind around you wherever you are in the arena. And he can cancel it into a block too. He usually doesn't though so easy to stop.

With Exdeath crushed, Bartz's Chocobo feather turns into his Crystal...I'm not sure why, what emotional trial did he pass again? He learned to stop running from enemies? He was in the Land of Discord, you don't stick around to fight when about half the villains in the game are in your way. Zidane then comes back and Bartz runs off to show him the real deal this time, so even the game says he hasn't learned a thing. Confusing
