The Reports

As you play through the game, you'll receive notification of new Reports being unlocked. Reports provide backstory for the world of Dissidia and also contain scenes of other characters our heroes don't see. But there's also fights to be done too. You can do the Reports as you unlock them or put them off, or not do them at all. I'll assume you did them as soon as you unlocked them because that's what I did, and if you're not going to do what I tell you then why are you here? I am your lord and master, you will play as i decree it! KNEEEEEL BEFORE DRAKE!

Report 01

First, go to the main menu and check the PP Catalog, and purchase Kuja for usage.

View the scenes of the first report, showing us Kuja decided to betray Chaos and help Zidane and his friends get to the god to fight him, but Kefka caught wind of the scheme and derailed it. Kuja has to pretend he was just putting on an act, and engages the trio of heroes. We also get a glimpse of one of my all-time favorite characters in the series and of all fiction, but we'll wait to talk about her in detail next time. Before you view the scene leading up to the fight, be sure to equip Kuja to make it easier, buy him at a Rod and a Bronze Bangle at the shop (you'll have head and body armor for him already), and give him the Power Ring, Level 1 - 9 and HP = 100% for accessories. The menu recommends he be Level 5, I'll be doing it at Level 1. If you feel like grinding a bit, go ahead and fight a bit in Quick Battle to boost him up and familiarize yourself with his attacks, he can be tricky to master. Note that you need to purchase Kuja from the PP Catalog to be allowed to access these fights, if you didn't go do it now.

First up is Bartz, with a Squall Assist. Squall's ground Assist hurts a lot, but his aerial Assist is slow and easily avoided. He starts off with a full EX Gauge and will be absorbing EX Force at a higher rate as long as he stays in EX Mode. Don't get too close when he's in EX Mode because Goblin Punch is near impossible to anticipate and will hurt a lot since Kuja and Bartz are likely both at Level 1 (GP gets a damage buff when used on an opponent the same level as Bartz). When in the air he'll spam Holy, easily reflected. His HP attacks are Hellfire and Ragnarok Blade, Hellfire has low horizontal range and Ragnarok Blade has long start-up, so avoiding HP damage isn't too hard. The attacks you want to see often are Hazard Raid and Slide Shooter, two charging attacks that are easy to block and punish. His summon Kraken begins a 15 second countdown to the two of you swapping Bravery, land an HP attack as the counter gets to 2 to steal Bartz's Bravery and leave him with 0.

Next up is Squall, his accessories will give him a big boost to physical defense when he's close to dying, a pity for him all of Kuja's attacks are magical. He's got Zidane for an Assist. Squall's Solid Barrel and Beat Fang hurt a lot but otherwise his attacks are mostly magical and none of them are too dangerous, though Mystic Flurry can be a nuisance. He's harder to avoid hits from compared to Bartz but fortunately there's no EX Mode shannanigans to worry about, and ultimately I found him easier to handle.

Finally, it's Zidane, with Bartz as his Assist and the build Squall wished he had, giving a massive boost to magic defense when he's low on HP. Zidane is flat one of the best characters in the game and one of my mains, and I say this for reasons this fight may demonstrate if the AI is feeling competent - he's got fast and versatile Bravery attacks that do quite a bit of damage, and many of them link to his Meo Twister HP attack. He's otherwise got very flexible HP attacks with Stellar Circle 5 to pull you in on the ground, Free Energy to explode in your face at close range, Shift Break to create a blast of water right under your feet, and Grand Lethal to charge at you from mid-range. His summon is Bomb, which will reduce Zidane's Bravery to 0 when it explodes and damages your Bravery by the same amount, try to build up more Bravery than Zidane has or you'll get Broken.

The best way to deal with Zidane is to play defensively, let him use one of his attacks and as long as it isn't Booster 8, his charging ground attack (attack cue "Stings huh?"), you can block it and punish him. If you've gotten your EX Gauge charged up, feel free to use it to activate EX Revenge and stop a heavy HP attack he catches you in. Otherwise try to finish him with an EX Burst to avoid the defensive gain he gets from his boosters as you assault him near-death.

With Zidane dead, Kuja is left to contemplate his failure to betray Chaos, while Kefka plots how to punish him for the attempt. And now you know why Kefka brought him to fight Lightning.

Report 02

This time the story is a lot shorter and the fights simpler. Terra is still hiding out at the Dimensional Castle when she sees Manikins heading to Order's Sanctuary, and she fights them to protect Cosmos. Equip her first, you should have plenty of stuff for her to use. The first villain is a Imaginary Soldier, he uses Sonic Break and Rising Fang, the former is a mid-range ground combo and the latter is a midair leaping attack. His HP attack is Braver, it has short range and high charge-up so it's simple to avoid. Terra starts out with a good moveset, Blizzara and Thundara for midair and Blizzard Combo and Fire for ground, and Tornado as her HP attack. She can easily mop up these foes with that moveset, she's easy to get the hang of too to boot.

Next up, a Imitation Despot. He has Thunder Crest, which creates a field of lightning on the ground to trap and stun you for several seconds, and Dynamite in midair, which fires an orb of energy that sticks to walls and floors, and will also trap and stun you before exploding, it really hurts so don't get caught. His HP attack is the aerial Flare, fires out an orange fireball to float in place, when he uses it a second time the first Flare vanishes. Flare will deflect your attacks but otherwise just avoid the traps and stay away from walls to make it easier to do so.

Third, the Transient Witch. She has Knight's Blade, which either fires a quick barrage of bolts as she turns to track you, or a slow storm of more bolts with slower turning to track you. Her HP attack is Great Attractor, which has a long charge-up time before she fires a planet at you, when you see her begin to charge it use Thundara to hit her and stop her.

Last up, our strongest foe is fittingly a Phantasmal Harlequin. He has the same tricks you've come to expect from them, particularly after the real Kefka showed up, but a nasty accessory set. He's got a Hyper Ring for +5% damage, and a series of timed Boosters to give him escalating boosts as the fight goes on longer. At 30 seconds five of them trigger for a total multiplier higher than 9x, giving him almost a 50% damage buff, and at 45 seconds that doubles, then doubles with an extra .5 at 60 seconds, and at 90 seconds, that triples. So yeah, try to end this quick or you're in for a world of agony when those boosters start firing and his damage output is more than doubled.

For real fun, spend the three fights up to this one charging Terra's EX Gauge. Her EX Mode is her Trance form and lets her use Dualcast to cast all her spells twice in a row, except for Blizzard Combo which just deals more hits. Crush the image of Kefka with her epic EX Burst Riot Blade, it's easy to input. Also enjoy the fun of "Smurf Terra", on the Wiki IRC we joked about Terra turning blue for her EX Mode when we saw her third outfit in trailers for 012, then we turned out to be right.

Now with the Manikins vanquished, Terra stumbles to Order's Sanctuary and collapses, weakened. In spite of her alignment, Cosmos senses her pure and good heart and imbues her with her power to awaken as one of her warriors in the next cycle.

Report 05

We have a recommended level of 5 for this, Sephiroth was Level 4 due to my using him for some Quick Battles previously. Of all characters to have a chat, here's Kefka and Sephy! Kefka tells Sephiroth that if two warriors from the same world fight, their memories return faster, and he wants to get Cloud off the bench and into the game, so to speak. So what's to do? There's a particularly girl on Team Cosmos Cloud knows from their world, if Sephiroth were to attack her, Cloud would run to the rescue and Sephiroth could punish him for betrayal. So Sephy does just that and tracks down Tifa.

Tifa has a very interesting accessory set-up, don't know where she lost all them when we got to her story. She has an Iifa Dew, so when she hits 0 HP, her Bravery is converted into HP and she lives. However, if she's Broken when she hits 0 HP, it won't trigger. And then she has Zephyr Cloak and Heart's Ease, giving her small boosts to Bravery when she dodges or blocks an attack, and seven Near Loss boosters, for a total 17x multiplier, increasing the boosts from those accessories to +34% of her base Bravery. Tifa is armed with a good attack set-up, Beat Rush, Blizzard and Blizzara on the ground and Falcon's Dive and Dolphin Blow in the air, with an attack we didn't learn yet, Moonsault Kick, a charging kick through the air. She has all the HP attacks you picked up in her story, and she's also got decent timing on her feints. What ruins Tifa's difficulty in this fight is that she has nothing but a Leather Hat for equipment, no weapon or armor and so her HP, defense and attack suck. You should have no trouble doing massive damage to her with Sephiroth and can wipe out her piddling 1300 HP easily. Just make sure to Break her before dealing the final blow.

Now we cut to Part 2 of the Report. Kefka tracks down Cloud and lets him know what's going on. Suit up Cloud and launch the scene. Sephiroth prepares to kill Tifa when Cloud arrives, and Sephiroth of course does what Kefka suggested. Somehow he's gained four levels from fighting Tifa because he's Level 8 now. Sephiroth has a whopping 99.9x booster here with a Hyper Ring...except his boosters only trigger when he's far enough away from Cloud, and even with his sword beam attacks he won't be far enough when attacking, so much like Sephiroth usually is, he's all show and no substance, his accessories are useless. He does have equipment though, missing a shield but having armor, a helmet and a sword, so he's more durable than Tifa. His attacks are easier than hers to block though, especially his ranged ones, so dodge or block them and punish him with Slashing Blow or Climhazzard, Cloud's Bravery attacks are ridiculously powerful. Sephiroth won't take long to put down.

In the aftermath, Sephiroth takes off and Cloud tells Tifa even though he saved her, he's one of the Chaos warriors, he just felt like saving her. Tifa recognizes her name, but Cloud denies that they know each other. We then get a few mythology gags, and Tifa muses that she and Cloud will have to fight each other eventually, and Cloud tells her to head back to the others before he has a change of heart. Tifa runs off and Cloud thinks she'd lose the will to fight when her memories return, so he has to do something before that happens.

Report 06

Time to find out what happened to Kain when we left him behind. I bought him accessory slots for a total of 6 and gave him a Power Ring, Hyper Ring, Pearl Necklace, HP = 100%, BRV ≥ Base Value, and Level 10-19. The game says its recommended level if 16, I was 15 so hey, pretty spot on. Exdeath unleashes a swarm of manikins against us we must fight off! In a neat touch of irony, they're manikins of the people we've seen Kain attack.

First up is a Fallacious Wanderer, incredibly pathetic, I stood there and he only ever used two attacks, crush him. Next, an Imitation Liegeman. He's a new foe and has Rope Knife, a mid-range attack that will pull you towards him for one of three follow-up attacks, watch his movements and block it when you see him make his move. Rope Knife can snag you on an angle, so he's liable to pull you out of the air and ensnare you if you're on a lower ledge. In the air he has Swordslash, a very quick but very short range sword blow that slams you down. He's more dangerous than the Wanderer by virtue of actually trying to hurt you, but still won't put up much resistance.

Third, a Capricious Thief, a Strange enemy. He's got Soul of the Craven for 0 starting Bravery, and three HP = 1 boosters with a Guardian Bangle for a pretty impressive defense bonus. He uses the Carbuncle summon to share his 0 Bravery with us, then spams Solution 9, firing a barrage of energy runes at us. It's actually an impressive set-up they gave him. Solution 9 has to be blocked several times since of course it hits several times, and attempting to dodge just has us get hit by the others he's firing. He's also got Scoop Art, firing three slow energy blasts we can reflect back at him. You might be inclined to just attack and Break him, but of course he's got that Guardian Bangle and a 64x booster. It's a bit hard to Break him even if he is at 0 Bravery when our Bravery attacks do 0 damage. Go ahead, combo the little sucker and see how well it works out for you. I even hit him with Spiral Blow, with 20 power the strongest single hit Kain can deal, no dice. The only way to beat him is to land an HP attack to get your Bravery back to normal, then deal the proper finishing blow. Jump is the perfect move here since he kinda can't move when using either of his attacks, and you'll move too fast for him to hit you mid-attack.

Next a Fleeing Flash, she actually has a powerful weapon and a Hyper Ring backed up by 8 HP = 100% boosters. Why is it these manikins are better equipped than the bosses we killed in the Epilogue? She's got Watera, Ruin, Ruinga, Launch, and an Assist - an Imaginary Brawler. She can be a mild challenge, deal an HP hit early on to deactivate her boosters and you won't have much trouble with her, as always the AI's incompetence ruins her. Now suddenly, the manikins stop attacking! But only a Warrior of Chaos can control them...Golbez! Kain gets past them and confronts the tree directly.

Contrary to that moment of treachery, Golbez is on Exdeath's side as his Assist here. The tree has two Hyper Rings and eight boosters to trigger when he's about to die, so watch out for a huge power boost when that comes. He's also got the Iron Giant summon and good equipment all-around, making him much more formidable than the five bosses the rest of the 012 crew faced down. He's got the same Omni Block, Reverse Polarity and Turn Guard tricks as before, but is comparatively not much more difficult, he's a few levels higher, but then Kain likely is to, and you've got better equipment for him this time. To avoid his power surge on the brink of death, either weaken him and blast him with an EX Burst to finish him, or build up enough Bravery to knock off the last chunk in one swoop without him entering near death status. Exdeath fades away and we get an excellent scene between Kain and Golbez I won't spoiler. It just serves to confirm for you doubters that Kain is a genuine hero.

Report 08

We take control of Shantotto now. Well, might as well put these here!

PLAYABLE CHARACTER - Shantotto

Remember I said Tifa and Vaan are two of the four characters I dislike and don't use? Shantotto is #3. Shantotto is really fun to watch in cutscenes and has a good vocal performance, and I like her as a character. She's just a horrible fighter. Her gimmick is creative but it doesn't work out. In the original Dissidia Shantotto was one of two secret characters unlocked after completing the story mode for her, she had little to do with the main story. Here it's much the same, though they are least do properly tie her story into the main plot by explaining how she ended up outside the cycles as she did.

Shantotto's Bravery attacks are supportive, she only has two proper attacks that do damage, and they're good for what they are. Otherwise she uses Bind, creative a binding ring of energy that holds the opponent in place, but they can still dodge and attack, just can't move. Stun interrupts the opponent's current action, and if it hits can chain into other attacks like Exdeath's Reverse Polarity. And finally Bio tosses out a cloud of poisons that tracks the opponent, and deals damage over time when it hits.

Shantotto's true strength is her HP attacks. She "levels" them up at 2000 and 4000 Bravery, and they hit over larger areas and do more damage, etc. Furthermore she can chain three HP attacks together, piling up more Bravery damage until the third hits for HP damage. It's a creative gimmick like I said, the problem comes in well...think about it. Against a competent, well-equipped opponent, how do you expect to build up 4000 Bravery? Even 2000? So that means most of the time you'll be using the Level 1 versions of her attacks, which are alright on their own but are still not that awesome once you figure out how to avoid them.

Maybe I just suck with her, maybe I just lack the patience to use her, but I dislike Shantotto honestly. She's annoying to fight as an AI, they're always Stunning you and them smashing her staff into your face, but annoying does not equal powerful. As a footnote they didn't do her any favors by nerfing her EX Mode - in Dissidia she could spam HP attacks freely because her EX Mode ability didn't lower her Bravery back to 0 after dealing HP damage, which also meant she could use an EX Burst and not only do extra damage because she kept the Bravery from the initializing HP attack, but she would still keep the Bravery she racked up from the Burst after it ended. In D012, it just restores her Bravery to its base value quickly after landing HP damage...basically it's just the Mandragora summon in that aspect, and by the way, the Mandragora summon sucks.

PLAYABLE CHARACTER - Gabranth

Gabranth is the other secret character from the original Dissidia, and like Shantotto he's an outsider to the cycles, he participated in them in the past but escaped somehow. For both of them, the how is explained in the Official Quests if you'd like to read.

Unlike Shantotto, Gabranth is 100% pure, unfiltered badass. His Bravery attacks suck, they're slow and weak. But that's fine, because his HP attack is unique - it does no damage, it just charges his EX Gauge. Once Gabranth fills that meter up, he enters EX Mode, the helmet goes on, the swords separate, and the carnage begins. Gabranth's moveset changes entirely in his EX Mode and he is insanely powerful, his Bravery attacks do massive damage, execute fast, have surprising range to them for melee combos, and a lot do Wall Rush for more pain. He also gets a big movement speed boost, and he gets the normal EX Mode benefits like Regen and a critical hit rate increase. And of course, an EX Burst. Gabranth's EX Burst is one of the coolests of the game. His HP attacks are mediocre, the only one really awesome is the aerial version of Innocence, sending a rain of giant energy blades crashing down over the arena. Hatred has its uses too though, and Execution...well I guess it doesn't hurt to round out the arsenal.

Gabranth is awesomely strong, he has three glaring weaknesses though. The first is, obviously, filling up that EX Gauge and keeping it active while you slice the opponent into red paste. His exclusive equipment, the Chaosbringer and Highway Star, help by increasing the duration and giving him more EX when he absorbs EX Force, and he can of course use accessories to power up both traits further. But ultimately, unless you rely on Exp to EX, you're gonna run out of power sooner or later, and Gabranth goes back to normal and you have to dodge the opponent using EX Charge when you get a break until you're back at full. The second is Assists, EX Charge slightly kinda very makes Gabranth a sitting duck, and if he gets hit with an Assist in EX Mode, then his gauge depletes. Fortunately the opponent gets no Bravery thanks to a unique ability of Gabranth, but still, no more EX Mode. Third and last, he has pretty much no ranged attacks, he has Gaia Breach which doesn't suck, but still isn't all that great and is just there to give him a ranged option. Gabranth isn't that good competitively when you fight without equipment and the opponent is a human who knows to just avoid the Judge until his EX Gauge depletes, but I still love him, he's a blast to play with. You know how in say, Kingdom Hearts Birth By Sleep, you entered a Command Style and just started pressing X to unleash hell until the Unversed stopped twitching? That's Gabranth's EX Mode. Go forth, ye players, and deliver judgement unto the hapless AIs.

Suit Shantotto up and launch the scene, in an earlier cycle of war, it was Shantotto who defended Cosmos, and Gabranth came to challenge her. Shantotto is so funny, it takes talent to be that snarky in rhyme. "So striking and tall, I think I'll incinerate you, pretty armor and all!" Gabranth is Level 10 and your Shantotto is probably Level 1, but he's still a pathetic opponent because, as you might guess, he needs to charge up that EX Gauge. He needs to hold still. Show him why that's a bad idea. The fight won't take long, just be sure to grab the EX Cores before he does. And if he tries to attack out of EX Mode, you can easily block and punish him.

With Gabranth out of the picture, an old friend runs up to Shantotto, Prishe! We'll do her character box later when we take control of her, Gabranth is an exception since he's not playable in any story mode fights. Prishe uses the pretence to "patrol" to go and explore, and Shantotto isn't pleased that this time it got Gabranth a free pass into the Sanctuary. Prishe ones off again as Shantotto snarks.

The next item of the Report is a scene, a certain friend has just wandered into the realm looking for his rival. Sadly, they just miss each other. But next scene, they meet! Suit up Bartz and prepare for destiny, for GILGAMESH has arrived! He is here seeking the rematch he promised Bartz, but Bartz has no memory of him. Well, he is here and he wants a fight, so let's give him one! In spite of the level difference Gilgamesh goes down pretty easily, and is promptly swallowed back into the Rift. Easy come, easy go.

Report 09

In the 13th cycle, Terra awakens alongside the Onion Knight and the two are quickly attacked! The first enemy is naturally a Counterfeit Wraith, it uses the spin attack tentacle attack on the ground and the energy orb barrage attack in the air, and has Anti-Air Particle Beam. Nothing even vaguely resembling a threat here. Next is a Phantasmal Harlequin, it as a Hyper Ring and Guardian Bangle with 8 HP = 100% boosters, full equipment, and uses Ultima, the three-blast Firaga, Scatter-Spray Blizzaga, Lickity-Split Thundaga, and for HP has Havoc Wing, Trine and Hyperdrive. Just land an HP attack early to sap its boosters and it isn't a problem.

After the fight Terra thanks the Knight for protecting her and begins to head off on her journey, but the Onion Knight knows she dislikes fighting and volunteers to come with her and protect her. Terra doesn't want to be a burden because of her hesitation, but he has none of it and tells her to just lay low if a fight comes. Terra muses the words sound familiar but doesn't remember why.

This unlocks the next Report, where in the 12th cycle, Vaan acts not only like an idiot, but an asshole, to Onion Knight, telling him since he's young he shouldn't work as hard as the "adults" and thinking it must be tough being treated like a kid yet still being called to fight. Onion Knight of course isn't treated like a kid by the other warriors, yes he's young but he's still strong enough to be called by Cosmos and he's perfectly capable in battle - not to mention that as he says, Vaan is a kid himself not that much older. Vaan of course ignores him and appoints himself the Knight's "big bro". We then get a couple scenes of Vaan trying to act like his guardian brother while the Onion Knight just gets annoyed, Vaan telling him he doesn't need to train so hard and yelling at the Warrior of Light for not going easy on Onion Knight when sparring. Vaan finally notices he's in a bad mood and asks what's the cause, assuming he's annoyed over getting hurt during that sparring match, but Onion Knight just sits there looking at him like he's a fucking idiot, which he is. However, he confides to Squall he's getting used to having someone look out for him...which Vaan isn't, he's being a condescending douche who thinks Onion Knight can't take care of himself.

So yeah, in the original Dissidia Onion Knight just wanted to protect Terra and their friendship was a neat throwback to Terra's kids at Mobliz. Dissidia 012, he's just Vaan's little brother and replaces him as Terra's guardian.

God how I wish Vaan wasn't in this game. It's a mighty feat when he manages to mangle two character arcs and still not have one of his own.

Report 16

For this one the recommended level is 34, I went in guns blazing with my level 19 Warrior of Light, let's rock! First is a level 30 Imaginary Brawler with Waterkick, Moonsault Kick, Burning Arrow, and 8 Near Death boosters with a Protect Stud. She was easily wiped out. Second is a Level 34 Ephemeral Summoner, she has Aerospark, Impulse, Energy Ray, a White Cape with 8 Near Death boosters, and the Barbarricia summon. That is one of the worst in the game kiddies because it is easy to turn back - Barbara appears when you call your own summon, and swaps your Bravery with the summoner's. Though with the Cactuar summon it won't do much, with other summons it can cripple the summoner and leave us supercharged. Anyway, the Summoner is also quickly dispatched.

The last foe is an Idle Sky Pirate, Level 38. He's got Axe & Shield, Katana, Greatsword, Staff, Rifle, Torrent and Inferno. His summon is Magic Pot, a Fleeting Flash is his Assist, and for accessories he's got a Hyper Ring with 4 HP = 100% and 5 BRV ≥ Base Value. He's the toughest foe, but only because he has a hand equipment piece to give him a defense bonus, otherwise, still easy to kill, even if he does have 15 or more levels on the Warrior. Crossover - Rune Saber for the win!

After the fights there's a short scene of Terra thanking the Warrior for protecting her, and then we unlock the next part of the Report. Now we take control of the Onion Knight and Cecil. The recommended level is 35, yeah and how did that go just a minute ago? Anyway, Cecil makes a crack that the Onion Knight has fallen for Terra, and the Warrior tells him if he's vowed to protect her he must stick with it like a true knight.

These three fights take place in one of the worst arenas in the game, Sky Fortress Bahamut Ω. Here rushing winds constantly push you back towards the end of the stage, where the entire wall is a banish trap back to the center area. This makes aerial combat virtually impossible since characters are constantly getting blown around. You'll see what I mean. It isn't a huge problem for the Onion Knight or Cecil really, but characters like Kuja, Zidane, even Terra and Kain, hate this arena. The Bravery pool rises when the characters keep their feet on the ground, if they're both grounded it rises faster.

Report 19

Savor this kiddies, the last Report fight! The recommended level is 50 for our hero, Gilgamesh. He arrives in the Rift to confront Bartz like we saw earlier, but Bartz curiously reacts a bit differently then we saw. Gilgamesh wants his promised rematched, and Bartz is happy to give it to him.

Bartz is Level 50 with good equipment and a pretty cool EX Mode build, watch out once you Break him.
